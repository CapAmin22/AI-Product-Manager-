AttributionOS: The Attribution & Budget-Intelligence Swarm

The reconciliation layer between what the platforms claim and what Finance actually banked: a supervised multi-agent system that computes blended truth, runs MMM-lite with confidence intervals, and proposes budget reallocations — every recommendation eval-gated and human-approved, never auto-executed.

Role: AI PM — Amin

01The Thesis

The Problem

All Wrong, Differently

Every platform claims credit for the same overlapping conversions, so summed platform-reported revenue routinely exceeds what Finance actually banked. Up to ~20% of purchases are influenced by creators, PR, and dark social no pixel sees. Quarterly budget allocation becomes a negotiation between dashboards that are all wrong — differently.

Strategic Pivot

Blended Truth + Trust Scores

No single pixel suffices. The credible fix is blended metrics (MER, blended CAC), MMM-lite with confidence intervals, and incrementality testing — reconciled nightly against Finance actuals, with a published per-channel trust score and every budget recommendation carrying its evidence.

The Methodology

The MMM eval no real-data project can run: The synthetic dataset (labeled synthetic everywhere) has known ground truth — true incremental contribution per channel, with channel-specific inflation factors baked into the "platform dashboards." The MMM is scored on how closely it recovers the known truth, and the recovery table is published.

Test the tester: The gate faces seeded bad recommendations — one based on a single outlier day, one exceeding the 20% policy cap, one contradicting the incrementality test, one favoring a low-trust channel on its self-reported ROAS alone. All four must be blocked with the correct reason, and a legitimate rec must pass.

Close the loop: Every approved recommendation stores its predicted lift; the next cycle joins realized outcomes. The realized-vs-predicted scorecard is what tells the human whether to trust the system more or less over time — and it's what separates this from every dashboard project.

05PM Judgment

The Hard Decision

Rejecting Auto-Execution

The tempting design was auto-execution — the demo is flashier and "autonomous" sells. Rejected: budget moves are irreversible within a cycle, accountability must sit with a named human, and an evaluator not yet stress-tested guarding real money is the textbook unsafe loop. Recommend-plus-approve, with a 20% per-cycle shift cap — and the saved effort spent making the human's 15 minutes count.

The Statistical Honesty Call

CIs, Never Point Estimates

A credible MMM wants 18–24 months of history; 90 days is directional. So every contribution ships as a confidence interval ("Meta ≈ 35% of incremental revenue, ±8%"), never a precise causal ROAS. Wide honest CIs beat narrow false ones — torturing the model until it looks precise is the hallucination this project exists to avoid.

The Trust Boundary

Agents Own

Ingestion & schema-drift handling, MMM + incrementality analysis, reconciliation math, drafting recommendations with evidence.

Human Owns

Choosing the source of truth (Finance wins), judging data sufficiency for MMM, approving every budget move, auditing the evaluator.

Anti-Rubber-Stamp

Overrides require a one-line reason — friction by design. Evidence shown before the ask; the gate's reasoning is never hidden.

07Retrospective

Test The Tester

The riskiest assumption in any loop is that your evaluator measures what you actually care about. An evaluator that agrees with everything is worse than none — seeded bad recommendations are how you prove the gate is awake before it guards anything that matters.

Memory ≠ A Long Transcript

Context accumulation isn't memory. Memory is structured outcomes joined back into decisions — approved recs with predicted lift, matched against what actually happened next cycle. That closed loop is what lets a human calibrate their trust in the system over time.
