BrandGround: The Grounded RAG Assistant

A retrieval-augmented assistant over brand guidelines + a competitor ad library — every answer carries inline citations, out-of-corpus questions are refused (not guessed), and its failure modes are documented, measured, and fixed.

Role: AI PM — Amin

01The Thesis

The Problem

~90%

Of enterprise AI products are RAG — yet brand knowledge stays scattered across PDFs, decks, and wikis, and generic AI confidently invents answers about it. Teams can't quickly answer "is this on-brand?" or "how does competitor X frame feature Y?" with a source they can trust.

Strategic Pivot

Honesty By Design

"I built RAG" is common. "Here are its failure modes and exactly what I did about each" is rare. BrandGround answers only from retrieved sources with inline citations — and when the corpus is silent, it abstains instead of confabulating. The teardown is the product.

The Methodology

Measure retrieval separately from generation: Context recall and precision diagnose whether the right chunk was even fetched; faithfulness catches hallucination; answer relevancy checks the answer addresses the question. One fuzzy "accuracy" number hides which half broke — most RAG failures are retrieval failures.

Break it on purpose: Each failure mode was deliberately induced — bad chunking, vocabulary mismatch on exact-term queries, out-of-corpus confabulation, multi-hop synthesis — then one fix applied and measured. Hybrid BM25 + vector search moved context recall from 0.68 to 0.86 on exact-term queries while faithfulness held at 0.91.

The ship rule: Faithfulness ≥ 0.9 on the eval set, and out-of-corpus questions must abstain rather than guess — demonstrated live in the demo, because on-camera honesty beats confidently answering everything.

05PM Judgment

The Hard Decision

The Chunking Call

800-token chunks with 120 overlap, chosen with evidence: smaller chunks split voice-and-tone guidance across boundaries and dropped context recall ~10 points. Chunking quietly decides whether RAG works — it's a justifiable product decision, not a library default.

The Rejected Alternative

Every Trick Up Front

Adding hybrid search, reranking, and semantic chunking all at once "to be thorough" means never knowing which one helped. One lever at a time, measured, kept or discarded. Reranking was skipped: its quality gain didn't justify the added latency and cost at this corpus size — a documented, defensible call.

The Trust Boundary

AI Owns

Retrieval + synthesis over a known corpus, with citations a human can verify in one click.

Not Trusted

Multi-hop reasoning across many docs, fast-changing data, anything outside the corpus — it confabulates. The honest "wrong tool" call is named.

The Rule

Answer only from numbered sources with inline citations; if the corpus is silent → "I don't have that in my sources."

06Economics

Cost Center Shape The Lever
Embedding the corpus One-time Cheap small-embedding tier; re-embed only on corpus change
Per-query generation Dominant recurring cost Cache frequent queries; tune chunk size against token cost (bigger chunks = more prompt tokens)
Reranking Optional latency + cost Only when the eval shows quality gain justifies it — here, it didn't

The Ownership Risk

A stale corpus makes the assistant confidently wrong as the world changes. Data freshness is a PM responsibility, not a code bug — the corpus needs an owner and a refresh cadence before this scales.

07Retrospective

Suspect Retrieval First

RAG doesn't make a model smart about your domain — it hands it the right page at answer-time. If retrieval hands it the wrong page, it answers wrong. Retrieval quality, not model size, is the lever; that diagnostic instinct is the whole edge.

Abstaining Builds Trust

The most impressive demo moment isn't a great answer — it's the assistant gracefully declining a question outside its sources. Designing for the model's real failure mode (confident confabulation) is worth more than answering everything.
